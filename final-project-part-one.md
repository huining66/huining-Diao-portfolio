## Executive Summary

Does working longer necessarily lead to a better life?

This research project explores the relationship between average annual working hours and subjective well-being across countries. Drawing on internationally available economic and happiness datasets, the project combines quantitative reasoning, exploratory data analysis, and visual storytelling to examine global patterns in work-life balance.

The objective is to identify meaningful associations, explore cross-country differences, and communicate economic and social insights through accessible data visualization.

## 01 — Research Background

Working hours represent an important dimension of labor market conditions, economic activity, and quality of life.

While longer working hours are often associated with greater effort and economic output, their relationship with human well-being is more complex. Differences in economic development, labor market institutions, social support, and cultural expectations may influence both working patterns and national happiness levels.

This project examines these relationships through an interdisciplinary approach that combines economics, data analytics, and information design.

### Central Research Question

> Do countries with longer average working hours tend to report lower levels of happiness?

### Supporting Research Questions

- What relationship exists between annual working hours and national happiness scores?
- How does this relationship vary across countries and geographic regions?
- Which countries deviate from the broader pattern?
- What economic and social factors might help explain these differences?

### Research Objectives

1. Integrate publicly available international datasets.
2. Examine the relationship between working hours and subjective well-being.
3. Identify cross-country patterns and potential outliers.
4. Translate analytical observations into clear and accessible visual insights.

| Annual Working Hours | Average annual hours worked per worker | Explanatory Variable |

The proposed data preparation process includes:

- Preparing the merged dataset for exploratory analysis

Python and pandas serve as the primary tools for data cleaning, transformation, and integration.

## 03 — Analytical Methodology

The research framework combines exploratory data analysis, comparative analysis, and visual communication.

### Exploratory Data Analysis

The initial analytical stage focuses on understanding the distribution and variation of working hours and happiness scores across countries.

Key considerations include:

- Variation in annual working hours
- Differences in national happiness scores
- Potential outliers and missing observations
- Cross-country data comparability

### Bivariate Analysis

A scatter plot serves as the primary visualization for examining the relationship between the two variables.

- **X-axis:** Average annual working hours
- **Y-axis:** National happiness score
- **Unit of observation:** Individual countries

This approach supports the identification of broad associations, clusters, and unusual observations.

### Cross-country Comparative Analysis

Country-level comparisons provide additional context for interpreting the relationship between working hours and happiness.

Particular attention is given to countries that deviate from the broader pattern, including those reporting relatively high happiness despite longer working hours.

These observations provide opportunities to explore potential economic, institutional, and social explanations.

## 06 — Research Limitations

### Correlation and Causation

An observed association between working hours and happiness does not establish a causal relationship.

### Cross-country Comparability

Differences in labor market structures, employment practices, and data collection methods may influence the comparability of national statistics.

### Aggregation Bias

National averages do not capture variation across individuals, occupations, industries, or demographic groups.

### Potential Confounding Factors

Income, economic development, healthcare, social support, employment conditions, and cultural differences may influence subjective well-being.

### Temporal Alignment

The reference years of working-hours and happiness observations should be aligned wherever possible to support meaningful comparisons.

These methodological considerations establish the boundaries of the research and guide the interpretation of subsequent findings.
