### Part II — Visualization Strategy & User Research

**Data Visualization · User Research · Information Design · Digital Storytelling**

## Executive Summary

Effective data analysis requires more than identifying patterns. It also requires communicating those patterns in a way that audiences can understand, interpret, and connect with.

Building on the economic research framework established in Part I, this phase translates the relationship between working hours and global well-being into a structured visual narrative.

Through storyboard development, exploratory visualizations, and qualitative user interviews, I examined how information design and narrative structure could improve the accessibility and interpretation of cross-country economic data.

The resulting user feedback informed a series of targeted design improvements for the final research publication.

## 01 — Visualization Strategy

The visualization strategy was developed around a central research question:

> Do longer working hours make people less happy?

Rather than presenting isolated charts, the project uses a structured narrative to guide readers from the initial economic question toward increasingly detailed analytical observations.

### Design Objectives

- Translate economic data into accessible visual information.
- Establish a logical progression from context to evidence.
- Communicate cross-country relationships and regional differences.
- Highlight meaningful observations without overstating causality.
- Improve chart comprehension through visual hierarchy and annotations.
- Incorporate audience feedback into the final design.

### Narrative Architecture

| Stage | Narrative Focus | Communication Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Opening Question | Establish relevance and engagement |
| 2 | Economic Context | Explain key variables and measurements |
| 3 | Main Data Comparison | Present the relationship between working hours and happiness |
| 4 | Country and Regional Insights | Explore notable observations and deviations |
| 5 | Conclusion | Communicate implications and key takeaways |

## 03 — Exploratory Data Visualizations

Three draft visualizations were developed to examine the research question from complementary analytical perspectives.

### Visualization 1 — Working Hours vs. Happiness

The primary scatter plot compares average annual working hours with national happiness scores.

Each observation represents a country, allowing readers to examine the direction and structure of the relationship between work intensity and subjective well-being.

<img width="537" height="360" alt="Scatter plot comparing annual working hours and national happiness scores" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/5eb808bc-1ef3-4cda-b9cf-34e00fd80091" />

*Figure 6. Draft scatter plot examining working hours and happiness across countries.*

**Analytical Purpose**

- Examine the association between working hours and happiness.
- Identify potential trends and clusters.
- Explore variation across individual countries.
- Identify observations that deviate from the broader pattern.

The visualization serves as an exploratory analytical tool rather than evidence of a causal relationship.

The regional comparison provides additional geographic context by examining average working hours and happiness across regions.

## 04 — User Research Methodology

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed visual narrative, I conducted qualitative interviews with three individuals who broadly matched the intended audience.

The research focused on chart comprehension, narrative clarity, audience engagement, and opportunities for improving information design.

### Target Audience

The intended audience includes college students and young professionals interested in:

- Work-life balance
- Career culture and working conditions
- Global quality-of-life comparisons
- Economic and social differences across countries

### Participant Profile

| Participant | Background | Age Group |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Participant 1 | Graduate Student | 20s |
| Participant 2 | Graduate Student | 20s |
| Participant 3 | Early-career Professional | 20s |

Participants were selected based on their relevance to the intended audience and the personal applicability of the research topic.

### Research Objectives

| Research Objective | Evaluation Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| Audience Engagement | Effectiveness of the opening research question |
| Chart Comprehension | Clarity and interpretability of the main visualization |
| Narrative Structure | Logical progression of the research story |
| Information Clarity | Identification of confusing or insufficiently explained content |
| Design Improvement | Opportunities to improve visual communication |

### Interview Protocol

Participants reviewed the draft storyboards and visualizations before providing qualitative feedback.

| Evaluation Area | Interview Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Initial Engagement | What is your first impression of this story or topic? |
| Visualization Comprehension | Is the main scatter plot easy to understand? Why or why not? |
| Narrative Flow | Does the order of the story feel logical? |
| Information Clarity | Was any part confusing or difficult to interpret? |
| Improvement Opportunities | What would make this story more engaging or clearer? |

## 05 — User Research Findings

The interviews indicated that the research topic was relevant and engaging to the intended audience.

However, participants also identified opportunities to improve chart interpretation, metric explanations, narrative pacing, and regional context.

### Participant Feedback

| Evaluation Area | Participant 1 | Participant 2 | Participant 3 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Initial Impression | Interesting and relevant topic | Relatable and engaging | Strong opening question |
| Scatter Plot Comprehension | Generally understandable; trend line would help | Additional labels and annotations needed | Clear overall; highlighted examples would help |
| Narrative Flow | Logical progression | Mostly logical | Logical, but middle section could be shorter |
| Information Gaps | Happiness score definition unclear | Outliers needed more explanation | Regional differences needed context |
| Recommended Improvements | Add a trend line | Add metric definitions | Add regional grouping or color coding |

### Key Research Insights

#### Insight 1 — The Research Question Resonated with the Audience

All three participants responded positively to the topic and its relevance to work-life balance.

This feedback supported retaining the central research question and the overall narrative direction.

#### Insight 2 — Visual Guidance Could Improve Chart Interpretation

Participants identified trend lines, annotations, and highlighted examples as potential improvements to the main scatter plot.

These suggestions indicated that the visualization could benefit from additional guidance without substantially increasing visual complexity.

#### Insight 3 — Metric Definitions Required Greater Clarity

The definition of the happiness score was identified as an area requiring additional explanation.

Clearer metric definitions would help readers interpret the comparison more accurately.

#### Insight 4 — Country and Regional Context Could Strengthen Interpretation

Participants requested more explanation of notable countries and regional differences.

This feedback suggested that additional contextual information could make the analysis more meaningful.

#### Insight 5 — Narrative Pacing Could Be Improved

One participant suggested shortening the middle section.

This highlighted an opportunity to improve the balance between explanatory text and visual evidence.

## 06 — Research Synthesis & Design Recommendations

The qualitative findings were translated into actionable design recommendations for the final publication.

| User Research Finding | Proposed Design Improvement | Intended Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Limited visibility of the overall trend | Add a fitted trend line to the scatter plot | Improve interpretation of the relationship |
| Insufficient explanation of outliers | Annotate notable countries | Strengthen country-level understanding |
| Unclear happiness score definition | Include a concise metric explanation | Improve analytical transparency |
| Text-heavy middle section | Reduce text and improve transitions | Improve narrative pacing |
| Limited regional context | Introduce regional color coding or segmentation | Support geographic comparisons |

These recommendations establish a direct connection between observed user needs and proposed design changes.

### Design Iteration Framework

| Stage | Activity |
| :--- | :--- |
| Research | Establish the economic question and analytical framework |
| Design | Develop storyboards and draft visualizations |
| Evaluation | Conduct qualitative user interviews |
| Synthesis | Identify recurring usability and communication challenges |
| Iteration | Translate feedback into targeted design improvements |
| Publication | Present the refined research narrative |

---

## 07 — Research Limitations

The user research was exploratory and involved three participants.

Although the interviews provided actionable feedback, the sample size does not support statistically generalizable conclusions about the broader audience.

Participants also shared relatively similar age and career-stage characteristics, which may limit the diversity of perspectives represented.

The findings are therefore used to guide design iteration rather than establish definitive measures of usability or audience comprehension.

In addition, the proposed trend line and visual comparisons are intended to support descriptive interpretation. They should not be interpreted as evidence that working hours directly cause changes in happiness.
